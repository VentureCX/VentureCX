# Pine Plains land pays taxes, not mortgages

**Bottom line:** The "buy acreage, roughly break even, own land" plan works in Pine Plains only if you pay cash or put more than half down. It does not work with ordinary 25–35% down financing. The models below use late-2026 rates of about 7.1–7.5%. On them, a 43.69-acre parcel like the Hicks Hill Road comp loses about **$33,000–$45,000 a year** when financed, and comes within a few hundred dollars of breaking even when bought with cash and leased to a farmer under the agricultural assessment. The reason is structural. Dutchess cropland rents for about **$54/acre/yr**, while local raw land sells for **$8,000–$21,000/acre**, so farm rent yields about 0.3–0.5% on price against borrowing costs of about 7%. A house on acreage rented long term does better, at about 3.5% before debt, but still cannot cover a mortgage at 25–35% down. You would need about 59% down, or rent of about $5,400/month against local comps of $3,500–$4,000. The ways to recover capital are selling development rights, which has paid about $7,100–$13,500/acre in Dutchess, and possibly a solar lease. Both are slow, competitive and politically constrained. The strategy works best as a lower-leverage store of value that pays its own carrying costs, plus an option on a later easement sale. As a self-funding leveraged investment, it fails. Land values were flat to soft in 2026, so appreciation will not close the gap quickly. This is general research, not financial advice. Every listing status and several inputs below are unverified and are flagged as such.

## Financed land loses $33K–$45K a year; cash land nearly breaks even

**Rates (as of Nov 12, 2026):** the Freddie Mac 30-year fixed averages **7.11%** and the 15-year **6.45%**, up from 6.24% a year earlier, after peaking near 7.4% in October ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms); [WCAX/AP](https://www.wcax.com/2026/11/05/average-us-30-year-mortgage-rate-falls-718-this-week-its-third-straight-decline/)). Raw land loans usually run **6.5–10% with 25–35% down** (aggregator sources: [Farmonaut](https://farmonaut.com/?p=129217), [Mossy Oak](https://www.mossyoakproperties.com/how-finance-land-best-loan-options-tips/)). Farm Credit East publishes no rates, but it paid **1.25% patronage** on 2025 loan volume ([Farm Credit East](https://www.farmcrediteast.com/about/NewsRoom/press-releases/260323-FarmCreditEastReportsStrong2025FinancialResults)). The base case below is therefore a **7.5%, 20-year land loan**, my estimate of a Farm Credit or local bank quote. Get a real quote from the Millbrook branch.

Bought with cash, the land almost pays for itself, but only with a qualifying farm tenant. **The ag assessment is worth about $6,900 a year here, nearly three times the lease rent.** The financed cases are a different kind of investment. At 25% down, the holder puts in about $38K a year on top of the $129K down payment. To break even on cash flow, the parcel would need to earn about **$915/acre/yr** ($40,000 ÷ 43.69). Only solar pays rates like that. To break even on total return instead, the land would need to appreciate about 7.3% a year ($37,881 ÷ $517,500). That is above the 3–5% statewide NASS trend, and well above the flat local trend in 2026. Leaving the cash case's equity in Treasuries or a CD would also earn several percent a year. That opportunity cost is the true cost of the "cash break-even" case, though no yield was sourced here.

## A rented farmhouse earns 3.5% before debt and needs 59% down to cover it

Long-term rent is the only steady income large enough to matter. Pine Plains houses list for **$2,775–$4,000/month**. Examples are 3411 Route 199 (3BR farmhouse, $4,000, April 2026), 1517 Route 83 (3BR on 9.59 acres, $3,995) and 82 Lake Rd (4BR, $3,500) ([Apartments.com](https://www.apartments.com/3411-ny-199-pine-plains-ny/rwyx581/); [Corcoran](https://corcoran.com/listing/for-rent/1517-route-83-pine-plains-ny-12567/92434601/regionId/106); [Homes.com](https://www.homes.com/pine-plains-ny/12567/houses-for-rent)). The template is **328 Round Lake Rd, Milan**: about 11.9 acres, a 3BR/1BA 1,880 sf house and a horse barn, asking **$649,000** ([Redfin Milan snippet](https://www.redfin.com/city/30351/NY/Milan); listing status unverified).

The model uses these assumptions:

- **Rent and vacancy:** $3,500/month, less 6% vacancy, for **$39,480 effective gross**.
- **Taxes:** 1.55% × $649,000 = **$10,060**. This uses the Pine Plains rate as a proxy; Milan's rate was not researched. The ag assessment would not cover the house or homesite, and STAR does not apply to a rental ([NYS STAR](https://tax.ny.gov/pit/property/star/exemption-amounts/star13.htm)).
- **Insurance:** $2,500 (assumed).
- **Maintenance and capital reserve:** 10% of rent, or $4,200 (assumed). The owner self-manages.

That gives **NOI of about $22,720, a 3.5% cap rate**. Investment-property loans were modeled at 7.75%, which assumes a premium over the 7.11% owner-occupant average.

Two breakeven checks show how far off this is. First, the largest loan the NOI can carry is $22,720 ÷ ($41,846 ÷ $486,750 = 0.08597) = **$264,278**, which means about **$384,700 (59%) down**. Second, at 25% down the rent would have to satisfy 12R × (0.94 − 0.10) = $41,846 + $12,560, which gives **R ≈ $5,397/month**. That is about $1,400 above the best local comp. A rentable cottage or ADU at about $1,700–$2,200/month ([Rentometer Dutchess](https://www.rentometer.com/average-rent-in/county/dutchess-county-ny)) could close most of that gap. A house plus a legal second unit is therefore the most realistic financed break-even structure.

Short-term rental does not fix the math. AirROI puts the 27 active Pine Plains listings at **$51,036 average gross, $483 ADR and 38.6% occupancy** (trailing 12 months, updated Nov 5, 2026). The median listing earns about $3,600/month ([AirROI](https://airroi.com/airbnb-data/united-states/new-york/town-of-pine-plains)), and AirDNA shows only about $35,400. Assume a rule-of-thumb 50–65% net after cleaning, management and fees, then subtract $12,560 of taxes and insurance. An average STR leaves about **$13,000–$20,600**, below the long-term rental. Even a top-quartile STR (about $82,600 gross × 60% − $12,560 ≈ **$37,000**) falls short of the $41,846 debt service at 25% down. It also carries regulatory risk, covered in the next section.

Solar is the only line on the table that could make a financed land purchase break even. It depends on things the buyer does not control. ### Ag assessment works for leased land, with conditions

Leased land qualifies for the agricultural assessment if it meets the **7-acre** test and either the farmer's operation averages **$10,000+ in gross sales** or the land is farmed under a **written lease of at least 5 years** alongside qualifying farmland. The owner files **RP-305 every year** by the taxable status date, which is usually March 1. Up to 50 acres of woodland per parcel can be included. A conversion triggers **5x the most recent year's tax savings plus 6% compound interest, with a 5-year lookback**, because all of Dutchess is in Agricultural District 21 ([NYS Tax Dept, updated Oct 30, 2026](https://www.tax.ny.gov/research/property/assess/valuation/ag_overview.htm); [Dutchess Ag Districts](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/Planning/Agricultural-Districts.htm)). The 2026 use values run from **$65 to $1,292/acre by soil group, with farm woodland at $478** ([2026 ag values memo](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/publications/orpts/current-year-ag-values.pdf)). The Pine Plains equalization rate is 100. The 2025/26 rate outside the hamlet districts is 2.11 county + 2.64 town + 0.74 fire + 0.39 library + 9.62 school = **$15.50 per $1,000, or 1.55%** ([Dutchess RPTSA 2025/26 rates](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/Real-Property-Tax/Docs/2026_Dutchess_County_Real_Property_Tax_Service_Agency.pdf)). A handshake "hay for taxes" deal with a small tenant can fail the sales test, so put the 5-year lease in writing. Wooded tracts of 50+ acres can instead use 480-a forest tax treatment. It cuts assessed value by up to 80% in exchange for a rolling 10-year commitment, a DEC management plan and steep penalties ([NYS 480-a manual](https://www.tax.ny.gov/research/property/assess/manuals/vol4/pt2/sec4_08/sec480_a.htm)).

### Zoning favors farming and long-term rental, and penalizes STR and solar speculation

Most acreage is zoned **Rural, with a 5-acre minimum lot and ADUs by special permit**. That comes from a third-party summary, because the town code on ecode360 blocked access ([offsite.camp](https://www.offsite.camp/zoning/dutchess/pine-plains/)). Subdivisions use conservation-subdivision design, which reduces lot yield ([2015 Zoning Law](https://www.pineplains-ny.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Zoning-Laws-All-through-July-2015.pdf)).

On short-term rentals, Pine Plains had **no STR law as of the latest sources**. The town attorney was drafting one modeled on Rhinebeck and Pleasant Valley in November 2025 ([Town Board minutes](https://www.pineplains-ny.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-11-20-Town-of-Pine-Plains-Town-Board-Meeting.pdf); [New Pine Plains Herald](https://www.newpineplainsherald.org/articles/community/pine-plains-opens-the-door-to-short-term-rental-regulations/)). Neighboring Stanford already regulates STRs ([Herald](https://www.newpineplainsherald.org/stanford-town-board-passes-short-term-rental-regulations/)).

The town's draft 2026 ADU tax exemption (RPTL §421-p) excludes ADUs that are not used for long-term housing. A public hearing was set for February 19, 2026, and adoption is unconfirmed ([draft local law](https://www.pineplains-ny.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Local-Law-Rescind-of-Local-Law-No.-3-of-2025-002.pdf)). ADUs funded by the state "Plus One" grant also cannot be used as STRs ([Herald](https://www.newpineplainsherald.org/pine-plains-town-board-takes-steps-to-relocate-town-hall-discusses-funding-for-housing/)).

For solar, the 2022 law allows small systems as accessory uses and sends commercial Tier 3 projects to special-permit review. A **Tier 3 moratorium** adopted in 2023 was last confirmed extended in January 2025 ([Local Law 3](https://www.pineplains-ny.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Local-Law-3-Solar-Moratorium.pdf); [CitizenPortal summary](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/9462532/New-York/Dutchess-County/Pine-Plains/Town-board-extends-solar-moratorium-while-drafting-revised-local-rules)). The one approved 42-acre project, Carson Power on Bean River Rd, drew an Article 78 lawsuit from neighbors ([Millerton News](https://millertonnews.com/pine-plains/pine-plains-oks-42-acre-solar-farm); [NY Courts](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2024/2024_50696.htm)). **Whether the moratorium is still in force, or was replaced, in late 2026 is unknown. Call the town clerk before pricing any solar upside.**

### Financing beyond a bank loan is mostly for real farmers

The cheapest debt is the FSA Down Payment loan. The borrower puts 5% down, FSA lends 45% at about 2.25%, and a commercial lender covers the rest. The direct Farm Ownership rate was 6.25% in October 2026 ([USDA FSA](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/news-events/news/10-01-2026/usda-announces-october-2026-lending-rates-agricultural-producers); the November rates are unverified). Both require **3 years of farm management experience** and have caps of $600,000 (ownership) and $300,150 (down payment) ([FSA](https://dts.fsa.usda.gov/programs-and-services/farm-loan-programs/farm-ownership-loans/index)). An investor who is not a farmer cannot use them, though a tenant farmer could buy land through them later. Farm Credit East's Country Living program covers rural home lots and homes with acreage on 5–30 year terms ([Farm Credit East](https://www.farmcrediteast.com/FINANCING/Rural-Home-Loans)), and its patronage rebate is the main edge over a bank.

## Easement sales are the real capital-recovery lever, for the right parcel

Selling development rights can return a large share of the purchase price. **Jordan Lane Farm in Pine Plains (153 acres) sold its easement for $1,090,063 in December 2020**, which is $1,090,063 ÷ 153 = **about $7,125/acre** ([NYSDEC](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NYSDEC/bulletins/2b12ec7)). **Chaseholm Farm (175 acres) had an estimated easement cost of $1,268,770**, about $7,250/acre; the date of that release is not confirmed ([Dutchess County](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/County-Executive/34100.htm)). In 2026, **Obercreek Farm in Wappinger (143 acres) drew about $1.93M** from county PMG and state money, about $13,500/acre in a higher-pressure part of the county ([Millerton News](https://millertonnews.com/partnership-for-manageable-growth-hits-5000-acre-preservation-milestone)). Funding is available. State FPIG Round 20 (October 20, 2026) awarded more than **$48.5M to 43 farms**, raised the maximum award to **$3M**, and still has **$15M+ available on a rolling basis** ([Governor](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-nearly-50-million-preserve-new-york-farmland)). Dutchess PMG matches up to 50% of project cost ([Dutchess PMG](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/Planning/Partnership-for-Manageable-Growth.htm)).

Applied to a 2026 purchase at about $11,800/acre, an easement at $6,000–$10,000/acre (my estimated range for Pine Plains) would return about **50–85% of the land cost**. The owner keeps the land, which can still be farmed, hunted and lived on, and it then carries permanently low ag value. The catches are serious. Awards go through a land trust sponsor, such as Dutchess Land Conservancy or Scenic Hudson, and favor larger working farms with prime soils that adjoin protected land. Closing typically takes 2–4+ years. A 43-acre parcel like Hicks Hill is probably too small for the state program, though its frontage on about 600 protected acres helps. Donating an easement instead gives a federal deduction of up to 50% of AGI with a 15-year carryforward, worth less in cash than a sale. The NY conservation easement credit is capped at **$5,000/yr** ([NYS DTF](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pit/credits/conservation_easement_credit.htm)). The resale value of eased land and land trusts' per-acre criteria were not found, so a buyer should talk to DLC before closing, not after.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Pine Plains land is not an income investment. It is a low-carry asset whose holding cost depends almost entirely on two administrative choices: a qualifying 5-year farm lease that cuts taxes by about 85%, and how much you borrow. Each 10 points of down payment on a $500K parcel moves annual cash flow by about $5,000, while farm rent moves it by about $1,600 at most. The workable version is to buy at or below the $8K–$12K/acre large-tract band, which a soft 2026 market with widespread price cuts makes possible. Pay cash or borrow very little. Lease to a farmer immediately to secure the ag assessment. Treat an easement sale (worth roughly 50–85% of purchase price, years out) and a solar lease as optional ways to recover capital, not as part of the base case. A buyer who needs leverage should use a house with a legal second unit for long-term rental, because no income source available to raw acreage can carry a 7% mortgage.

The less obvious risks sit in exit planning rather than the purchase. Ag-assessment rollback penalties, the perpetual resale restriction on eased land, and possible new STR and solar rules each reduce flexibility later. The Carvel estate's 1,946 acres could reset local land prices in either direction. Anyone pursuing this should get a Farm Credit East quote, a soil-group worksheet and a sit-down with Dutchess Land Conservancy before making an offer, because those three determine whether a given parcel breaks even. This is general research, not financial, tax or legal advice.
