# Tax and Legal Treatment of a Farm + Short-Term Rental (Airbnb) Farmhouse — Pine Plains, NY (Dutchess County)

Researched 2026-11-17. General information, not tax or legal advice. Many sources are CPA/firm blogs or secondary case summaries. Where I did not fetch a primary source (IRS publication, statute, opinion), I say so. Items marked "[statute/background, not fetched]" come from well-established law I know and should be checked against the cited primary source before anyone relies on them.

## 1. IRS hobby loss rules (IRC §183): presumption, 9 factors, recent Tax Court farm cases

### Takeaway
If the farm is held to be "not engaged in for profit," its losses cannot offset W-2 or STR income. Since 2018, and permanently after the 2025 OBBBA, hobby expenses get no deduction at all, while hobby income is still fully taxed. Recent Tax Court cases from 2025–2026 turn on recordkeeping, a written business plan, changing course after losses, and personal-pleasure elements. Most cases go to the IRS. Kolar (2026) is a notable taxpayer win.

### Cited Findings
- **Factors.** The court weighs the nine factors of Treas. Reg. §1.183-2(b). As listed in the Young analysis, they are: manner in which the activity is carried on; expertise; time and effort; expectation that assets will appreciate; success in similar activities; history of income or losses; amount of occasional profits; the taxpayer's financial status; and elements of personal pleasure. — [Current Federal Tax Developments, Young analysis (9/22/2025)](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/9/22/profit-motive-under-section-183-an-analysis-of-young-v-commissioner)
- **Presumption.** §183(d) presumes a profit motive if the activity shows a profit in 3 of 5 consecutive years. For horse breeding, training, showing or racing the test is 2 of 7. In Schumacher (T.C. Memo. 2026-47) the horse activity had not shown a profit in 2 of the last 7 years, so the safe harbor was unavailable. — [CFTD, Schumacher analysis (6/9/2026)](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/6/9/an-analysis-of-schumacher-v-commissioner-section-183-hobby-loss-and-section-6662-penalty-determinations). The 3-of-5 general rule is [statute/background, not fetched].
- **Hobby expenses are permanently nondeductible.** OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, §70110) made permanent the TCJA suspension of miscellaneous itemized deductions, which includes hobby expenses under §67(g), re-lettered §67(h). It applies to tax years beginning after 12/31/2025. — [SJSU/Annette Nellen, §67 OBBB changes](https://www.sjsu.edu/people/annette.nellen/67_OBBB_Changes.pdf); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/what-obbb-means-for-your-clients-itemized-deductions-2/)
- **Young v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2025-95 (IRS win).**
  - The property was an 82.7-acre "Pecandarosa Ranch" with pecans, hay and cattle, horses, team roping, and a 22,590 sq ft arena.
  - Losses were $257,656 (2013) and $305,893 (2014), and about $2.95M of ranch losses from 2008 to 2022 offset wage income.
  - The ranch used a personal bank account, kept no general ledger, and had no business plan until the audit began.
  - Six factors favored the IRS, one favored the taxpayers (time and effort), and two were neutral.
  - The 20% accuracy-related penalty was sustained. Relying on a return preparer alone was not reasonable cause.
  - The court treated the ranching operations as one activity but held the landholding separate.
  - Source: [CFTD](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/9/22/profit-motive-under-section-183-an-analysis-of-young-v-commissioner)
- **Himmel v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2025-35 (IRS win).** Losses from an Arabian horse operation were disallowed. The couple did not cut costs or change practices after repeated setbacks. — [CFTD, Himmel analysis (4/17/2025)](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/4/17/hobby-loss-and-failure-to-file-a-deep-dive-into-himmel-v-commissioner)
- **Kolar v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2026-15, filed 2/9/2026 (taxpayer win).**
  - An 800+ acre Texas family ranch shifted its focus to beef cattle with a plan to grow the herd past 250 head. The court allowed $205,514 of 2016 farm expenses.
  - Four factors favored the taxpayer, two were neutral, and three went against.
  - The taxpayer's full-time lifelong work, his lack of recreational involvement such as cattle shows, and his wife's detailed financial records all helped.
  - Large oil and gas royalty income weighed against him but did not control. A long startup period justified the losses.
  - The court declined to aggregate the farm with the landholdings.
  - Sources: [CFTD, Kolar review](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/2/9/profit-motive-established-a-technical-review-of-kolar-v-commissioner); [Journal of Accountancy (June 2026)](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2026/jun/tax-court-allows-cattle-ranch-deductions/); [Parker Tax](https://parkertaxpublishing.com/public/irs-ranch-for-profit.html)
- **Schumacher v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2026-47.** Horse losses were disallowed, but the 20% penalty was rejected. — [CFTD (6/9/2026)](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/6/9/an-analysis-of-schumacher-v-commissioner-section-183-hobby-loss-and-section-6662-penalty-determinations); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/u-s-tax-court-holds-horse-breeding-activity-lacked-profit-motive-rejects-accuracy-related-penalties)
- **Other cases surfaced but not reviewed in detail:**
  - Schwarz v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2025-122 — [CFTD (11/25/2025)](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/11/25/revisiting-the-profit-motive-standard-the-impact-of-schwarz-v-commissioner-tc-memo-2025-122)
  - Chapin v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2026-76 (8/27/2026) — [Freeman Law](https://freemanlaw.com/tax-court-in-brief-august-24-28-2026/)
  - A Journal of Accountancy March 2026 item, "Court determines taxpayer lacked profit motive" — [JofA](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2026/mar/court-determines-taxpayer-lacked-profit-motive/)
  - Bucci, a Second Circuit 2025 horse racing and farming case. The Second Circuit covers New York, but I did not confirm the outcome — [hallapproved](https://hallapproved.com/us/cases/ca2/2025/10336363/)

### Inferences
- **Lifestyle-farm risk.** A Hudson Valley "lifestyle" farm owned by a buyer with high W-2 income looks like the fact pattern the IRS wins: personal-pleasure elements, large outside income, and continuing losses.
- **Defenses.** Keep a separate bank account and books, write a business plan before losses begin, document changes made in response to losses, and keep hour logs.
- **Grouping the farm with the STR.** Kolar and Young show the court defining the "activity" narrowly or broadly. Taxpayers sometimes argue to treat the farm and STR as one activity so that STR profit helps the farm's profit history. Under Reg. §1.183-1(d)(1) that requires a close economic interrelationship [statute/background, not fetched]. It is uncertain, and it cuts both ways if the STR loses money.
- **Deliberate STR losses.** If the STR is designed to produce tax losses through cost segregation and bonus depreciation, it will not help a combined §183 profit history.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full Tax Court opinions. Holdings come from secondary summaries.
- I found no 2023–2026 Tax Court case specifically involving a farm combined with a farm-stay or STR.
- The Bucci outcome is unconfirmed.

## 2. Schedule F: deductions, depreciation, conservation, income averaging, SE tax

### Takeaway
- **Bonus depreciation.** OBBBA (signed July 2025) permanently restored 100% bonus depreciation for property acquired and placed in service after 1/19/2025.
- **Section 179.** The limit for 2026 is about $2.56M, after OBBBA set a $2.5M base with a $4M phase-out start, indexed.
- **Farm buildings.** General-purpose farm buildings are 20-year MACRS (150% DB), and single-purpose agricultural structures are 10-year. Both are bonus-eligible.
- **Loss limits.** The excess business loss limit (§461(l)) is now permanent. It can cap how much combined farm and STR loss offsets wages in one year.
- **Self-employment tax.** Schedule F net profit is subject to SE tax.

### Cited Findings
- **Bonus depreciation.** OBBBA permanently restored 100% bonus depreciation under §168(k) for property acquired and placed in service after Jan. 19, 2025, with no phase-down. — [Foster Garvey "Larry's Tax Law" OBBBA Part 10](https://www.foster.com/newsroom/blog/larrys-tax-law/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-part-10-one-hundred-percent-expensing-lives-on/); [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/obba-tax-bonus-depreciation.html). One aggregator described restoration "through 2029," but most sources, including RSM, say permanent. Treat "permanent" as the majority view.
  - Taxpayers can elect 40% instead of 100% for the first tax year ending after 1/19/2025. — [MSU/CRI summaries via search](https://www.criadv.com/insight/obbba-bonus-depreciation-section-179-changes/)
- **New 100% expensing for nonresidential production buildings.** OBBBA adds 100% expensing for "qualified production property," which is nonresidential real property used in production. Leased property and nonproduction portions are excluded. It is unclear whether farm production qualifies, and an STR clearly would not. — [Wiss](https://wiss.com/depreciation-obbba-tax-law-changes-2025/). I did not confirm farm applicability.
- **Section 179.** OBBBA raised the limit to $2.5M with a phase-out beginning at $4M, previously $1.22M / $3.05M. The 2026 inflation-adjusted figure is $2,560,000.
  - §179 is limited to taxable income from the active conduct of a trade or business. Bonus depreciation is not limited that way and can create an NOL.
  - Heavy SUVs are capped at $32,000 under §179 in 2026.
  - Sources: [SparkReceipt (2026)](https://sparkreceipt.com/blog/section-179-deduction-2026/); [UWorld](https://accounting.uworld.com/?p=189665)
  - Sources disagree on the phase-out figure. One search summary said $5M, but I believe the statutory figure is $4M [statute/background]. Verify against the 2026 Form 4562 instructions.
- **Farm building recovery periods.**
  - General-purpose farm buildings are 20-year GDS and 25-year ADS.
  - Single-purpose agricultural or horticultural structures are 10-year property.
  - 15- and 20-year farm property uses 150% DB, or a straight-line election.
  - Bonus depreciation is the default unless the taxpayer elects out, by class.
  - Sources: [Mississippi State Extension P3842 (2026)](https://extension.msstate.edu/sites/default/files/document/2026-04/P3842_web.pdf); [U. Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/ag-business-management-news/farming-retirement-maximizing-write-offs-and-avoiding-recapture-trap)
  - A farmhouse is residential rental or personal-use property, not a farm building.
- **Excess business loss limit.** OBBBA removed the 2028 sunset, making §461(l) permanent. Disallowed excess carries forward as an NOL.
  - The 2026 threshold is reported as $256,000 single / $512,000 MFJ, from a reset base of $250K/$500K. The 2025 figure was $313,000 / $626,000.
  - Sources: [Moss Adams (11/2025)](https://www.mossadams.com/articles/2025/11/obbba-and-excess-business-loss-limitations); [TSCPA](https://tscpatax.com/articles/excess-business-loss-limitation-2026); [Wolters Kluwer](https://wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/navigating-compliance-planning-and-advisory-opportunities-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act)
  - The exact 2026 number is not confirmed against IRS Form 461 instructions.
- **Soil and water conservation (§175).** Farmers may deduct these expenses currently, limited to 25% of gross income from farming, with carryforward of the excess. — [IRS Pub 225, Farmer's Tax Guide](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225) [statute/background, Pub 225 not fetched this session]
- **Farm income averaging (Schedule J, §1301).** An individual farmer may spread elected farm income over the prior three years' tax brackets. — [IRS Pub 225](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225) [background, not fetched]
- **Self-employment tax.** Schedule F net earnings are subject to 15.3% SE tax. The farm optional method lets low-income farmers keep earning Social Security credits. — [IRS Pub 225](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225) [background, not fetched]

### Inferences
- **Cash flow.** With 100% bonus depreciation and a large §179 limit, equipment, fencing, single-purpose barns, and general-purpose barns can be written off in year 1. That creates large early Schedule F losses.
- **Two risks from those losses.** They increase §183 scrutiny, and §461(l) caps the amount that offsets W-2 income in a given year.
- **Recapture.** Early write-offs produce §1245 ordinary-income recapture on sale.
- **State conformity.** New York generally decouples from federal bonus depreciation, except for qualified resurgence zone and NYLZ property, so NY addbacks are likely [background, not verified this session]. Confirm with NYS DTF Form IT-398.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the 2025 or 2026 Pub 225. The §175 limit, Schedule J, and SE-tax details rely on background knowledge.
- The 2026 §179 phase-out threshold and §461(l) amount need confirmation.
- I did not confirm NY decoupling from OBBBA bonus depreciation.

## 3. STR tax treatment: the "STR loophole," passive rules, cost segregation, allocation with the farm

### Takeaway
- **Not a "rental activity."** Under Treas. Reg. §1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A), a farmhouse STR with an average guest stay of 7 days or less is not a "rental activity" for the passive loss rules.
- **Material participation unlocks losses.** If the owner materially participates (e.g., 100+ hours and more than anyone else, or 500+ hours), STR losses are nonpassive. Losses accelerated by cost segregation and 100% bonus depreciation can then offset W-2 income, subject to §461(l).
- **Schedule E vs. Schedule C.** It remains Schedule E with no SE tax unless "substantial services" (hotel-like) are provided. Substantial services move it to Schedule C and SE tax.
- **Personal use.** If the owners live in the farmhouse, the §280A personal-use limits apply.

### Cited Findings
- **The 7-day rule.** An activity is not a rental activity if the average period of customer use is 7 days or less. The test applies year by year. A "period of customer use" is the period in which a customer has the right to use the property, not actual use.
  - A separate exception applies at 30 days or less when significant personal services are provided.
  - Sources: [eCFR §1.469-1T](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR53de09ff5f206e9/section-1.469-1T); [timbertax.org reg text](https://timbertax.org/research/regulations/26cfr1.469-1T)
- **Material participation.** Reg. §1.469-5T includes a test of more than 100 hours that is not less than any other individual's participation. — [Goshorn v. Commissioner via cetient](https://cetient.com/case/4602213/goshorn-v-commissioner). The 500-hour test and the others are background [IRS Pub 925](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925) [not fetched].
  - **Trap.** Hours by cleaners, co-hosts, and property managers count as "other individuals," so the owner can fail the 100-hour test.
- **The loophole needs both elements.** It requires a short average stay plus material participation, and real estate professional status is not required. — [WCG CPAs](https://wcginc.com/blog/short-term-rental-loophole/); [1-800Accountant](https://1800accountant.com/blog/short-term-rental-tax)
- **Schedule C vs. E.** Substantial services (daily cleaning during the stay, meals, concierge) push income to Schedule C with 15.3% SE tax. Ordinary cleaning between stays does not.
  - Sources: [Taxstra](https://taxstra.com/str-loophole/schedule-e-vs-schedule-c/); [Morkel Financial](https://morkelfinancial.com/blog/short-term-rental-loophole-material-participation); [TaxAudit (2025)](https://www.taxaudit.com/tax-audit-blog/2025/tax-pitfalls-and-loopholes-of-airbnb-and-short-term-rentals)
  - These are practitioner sources and disagree on edge cases. IRS Pub 527 covers this [not fetched].
  - **Agritourism risk.** A "farm stay" with farm breakfasts or guided activities moves toward "substantial services."
- **Cost segregation.** Cost segregation reclassifies building components into 5-, 7-, and 15-year property. Combined with OBBBA's permanent 100% bonus depreciation, this is the core of the STR strategy in 2026.
  - Large STR losses can be limited by §461(l).
  - Sources: [Beancount blog on STR + OBBBA (6/2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/05/short-term-rental-str-loophole-material-participation-tax-strategy-airbnb-vrbo-hosts-obbba-100-bonus-depreciation-cost-segregation-section-469-seven-day-rule-w2-offset-guide); [BiggerPockets](https://www.biggerpockets.com/posts/7277824) (both low-authority)

### Inferences
- **Allocation on a single parcel.** Basis has to be split among:
  - land, which is not depreciable;
  - the STR farmhouse, which is 39-year nonresidential property under the 7-day/hotel-like view. Practitioners debate 27.5 vs. 39 years: a dwelling unit is excluded from "residential rental" if more than half its units are used on a transient basis [background, verify];
  - farm buildings and land improvements, which are Schedule F property;
  - any owner residence, which is personal and not depreciable.
- **Allocation method.** Use an appraisal or the assessor's land/improvement ratio, and keep the split consistent with the property tax ag-assessment ineligible "residence lot."
- **Owner living in the farmhouse.** If the owner uses the farmhouse personally for more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of rental days, §280A limits deductions and the STR strategy largely fails. — [IRS Pub 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527) [background, not fetched]
- **Grouping.** The STR, which is not a rental activity, may be groupable with the farm under Reg. §1.469-4 as an "appropriate economic unit." That could help satisfy material participation across both [background, uncertain]. Practitioners differ, so a CPA should decide.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative guidance on whether a farm-stay STR's "farm experience" components count as substantial services.
- I did not fetch Pub 527 or Pub 925.
- The 27.5-year vs. 39-year question for a single STR house needs CPA confirmation.

## 4. NY property tax, sales tax, and farm tax credits

### Takeaway
- **Residence exclusion.** NY agricultural assessment (Ag & Markets Law §§305, 306) covers only farmland and certain support structures. Land associated with the owner's residence is expressly ineligible, as are the residence and other buildings themselves.
- **Conversion.** Converting ag-assessed land to a nonagricultural use, an "outward or affirmative act," triggers a payment of 5x the taxes saved in the last year plus 6% compounded interest. The lookback is 5 years inside an ag district and 8 years outside. Partial conversions are apportioned.
- **STR on the house lot.** An STR in the existing, already-ineligible farmhouse lot should not by itself be a conversion of ag land. Expanding guest-use areas onto ag-assessed acreage (parking, glamping sites, event lawn) could be.
- **Farm building exemption.** RPTL §483 gives a 10-year exemption for new farm buildings but excludes residences and inseparable mixed-use buildings.
- **Credits.** The NY farm credits (ITC refundability extended to 2033, Farm Workforce Retention Credit, Farmers' School Tax Credit) depend on "eligible farmer" status. That requires farm income of at least two-thirds of federal gross income minus $30,000, which large W-2 or STR income can defeat.

### Cited Findings
- **Ag assessment eligibility.**
  - 7+ acres with average gross sales of $10,000 or more over the prior 2 years, or under 7 acres with $50,000 or more. Start-ups can qualify in their first or second year.
  - Rented land qualifies under a written lease of 5 years or more.
  - Commercial horse boarding: 7 acres, 10 horses, and $10K in receipts.
  - Farm woodland is eligible up to 50 acres per parcel.
  - Sources: [NYS DTF ORPTS ag assessment overview](https://www.tax.ny.gov/research/property/assess/valuation/ag_overview.htm); [ORPTS presentation](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/ORPTS/ag-assessment-presentation-printable.pdf)
- **Residence exclusion.** "Land associated with the owner's residence is ineligible." The program covers land and certain support structures, not farm buildings, residences, or other improvements. On the application, the residence and the land supporting it are entered as ineligible land.
  - For an entity-owned farm, a shareholder or partner living on the land is treated as occupying an owner's residence.
  - Sources: [DTF overview](https://www.tax.ny.gov/research/property/assess/valuation/ag_overview.htm); [ORPTS legal opinion v3/113](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/orpts/legal_opinions/v3/113.htm)
- **Conversion penalty.**
  - Conversion is "an outward or affirmative act changing the use of agricultural land." Idling or abandoning land is not conversion, but the land loses eligibility.
  - The payment is 5x the taxes saved in the most recent year plus 6% annual compounded interest, capped at 5 years. The lookback is 5 years inside an ag district and 8 years outside. Partial conversions are apportioned.
  - The owner must notify the assessor within 90 days, or face a penalty of up to twice the payment, capped at $1,000.
  - Source: [DTF overview](https://www.tax.ny.gov/research/property/assess/valuation/ag_overview.htm)
- **RPTL §483 farm building exemption.**
  - It is a 10-year exemption for structures built or reconstructed between 1/1/1969 and 1/1/2029. They must be essential to farming on at least 5 acres in bona fide production. They must be used directly and exclusively to produce or store commodities for sale, or to house regular and essential farm employees.
  - Excluded: the applicant's or immediate family's residence ("Housing for the immediate family of the farm owner is expressly excluded"), retail stands, most processing, and buildings with inseparable mixed farm and nonfarm uses. Incidental nonfarm use may not disqualify.
  - The exemption equals the increase in assessed value. The owner files RP-483 within 1 year of completion.
  - Conversion or ceasing farm use triggers roll-back of the taxes saved.
  - Sources: [NYS DTF farm building exemption](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/orpts/farmbld.htm); [RPTL §483](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPT/483)
  - Sources conflict on how far roll-back reaches: one county says the whole exempt period, while an older ORPTS opinion says up to 5 years — [Clinton County](https://clintoncountyny.gov/node/516)
- **NY sales tax for farmers (ST-125).**
  - Form ST-125 (Farmer's and Commercial Horse Boarding Operator's Exemption Certificate) lets farmers buy qualifying items used predominantly in farm production tax-free. Motor fuel and diesel are handled separately.
  - Building materials for farm production buildings can be exempt through the contractor, with the farmer furnishing ST-125.
  - Items not used directly or predominantly in production do not qualify.
  - Sources: [NYS DTF farmer's exemption certificate](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/farmers_exemption_cert.htm); [TSB-M-00(8)S](https://tax.ny.gov/pdf/memos/sales/m00_8s.pdf); [Cornell Small Farms](https://smallfarms.cornell.edu/guide/guide-to-farming/sales-tax-exemptions-refunds)
  - STR furnishings and supplies would not qualify.
- **STR sales and occupancy tax (cost side).**
  - Effective March 1, 2025, NY state and local sales tax applies to all short-term rental occupancies. Platforms collect and remit.
  - Dutchess County Local Law No. 1 of 2025 raised the county occupancy tax from 4% to 5% effective 1/14/2025. Hosts must remit the 5% on STRs not booked through Airbnb.
  - Sources: [Dutchess County Hotel Occupancy Tax](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/Finance/Hotel-Occupancy-Tax.htm); [Dutchess 2025 sales tax revenue](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/Comptroller/Docs/2025-Sales-Tax-Revenue.pdf); [Bonadio](https://bonadio.com/article/new-york-states-expanded-sales-tax-for-short-term-rentals-what-hosts-need-to-know)
  - A December 2024 state law lets counties set up STR registries, with platform reporting every 90 days. — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/mylodgetax/en/blog/2025/1/new-york-state-law-gives-counties-tools-to-track-and-tax-short-term-rentals.html); [NYSAC](https://nysac.org/issues/short-term-rentals)
- **NY FY2027 budget, enacted 2026.**
  - The 20% refundable Investment Tax Credit for eligible farmers is extended to 1/1/2033 (previously end of 2027).
  - The farm donation credit rises to 50% of fair market value, capped at $20K.
  - A unified "eligible farmer" definition now covers the Farm Workforce Retention, Farm Employer Overtime, and Farmers' School Tax credits: NY gross farm income (current year or 3-year average) of at least two-thirds of federal gross income minus $30,000.
  - Source: [Farm Credit East (6/4/2026)](https://www.farmcrediteast.com/resources/todays-harvest-Blog/260604FarmHighlightsNYSBudget)
- **Farm Workforce Retention Credit.** $1,200 per eligible farm employee. Extended to tax years beginning before 1/1/2029. Reported to require 500+ hours of employment. — [NYS DTF](https://www.tax.ny.gov/bus/ct/farm_workforce_ret_credit.htm); [NYS TER FY27](https://www.tax.ny.gov/data/stats/ter/fiscal-year27/recent-legislation.htm)
- **Farmers' School Tax Credit.**
  - The state assembly says it covers 100% of school property taxes on the first 350 acres and 50% on the rest, subject to income-based limits. A third-party guide says 250 acres.
  - Source: [NYS Assembly agriculture summary](https://assembly.ny.gov/write/upload/req/agriculture.pdf)
  - It continues in 2026 under the new unified eligibility definition per [Farm Credit East](https://www.farmcrediteast.com/resources/todays-harvest-Blog/260604FarmHighlightsNYSBudget).
  - Only qualified agricultural property is eligible. The residence portion likely does not count [background; verify on IT-217 instructions].

### Inferences
- **Credits are probably out of reach.** For a buyer with significant W-2 income, or with STR income (which is not farm income), the two-thirds test will likely fail. In that case the NY ITC, Workforce Retention, and School Tax credits are probably unavailable.
- **Income mix.** STR revenue increases federal gross income without counting as farm income, which works against eligibility.
- **Ag assessment and the house lot.** Ag assessment can cut the land's taxable value substantially. The farmhouse and house lot stay at full assessment either way, so the STR does not lose a benefit it never had on that land.
- **Conversion risk on the acreage.** Converting ag-assessed acreage for guest amenities risks a 5x payback. Ask the Pine Plains assessor how a farm-stay's incidental guest use of fields is treated. I found no ORPTS opinion on point.
- **Dutchess sales tax rate.** Dutchess sales tax on STRs (4% state plus 3.75% local) is not verified in this session. Add the 5% county occupancy tax.

### Gaps
- I found no ORPTS opinion on whether STR or agritourism lodging on ag-assessed land is a conversion.
- The 350 vs. 250 acreage cap for the Farmers' School Tax Credit is unresolved. I treat 350 as authoritative.
- I did not confirm whether Dutchess has adopted a county STR registry or whether Pine Plains has an STR permit law. Nearby towns (Washington, Millbrook) have acted — [Millerton News](https://millertonnews.com/washington-and-millbrook-take-action-on-short-term-rentals).
- I did not verify that the Dutchess combined sales tax rate is 8.125%.

## 5. NY Agricultural District protections (AML §305-a), agritourism, farm stays, liability

### Takeaway
- **What §305-a protects.** It shields "farm operations" (AML §301(11)) in a state-certified agricultural district from unreasonably restrictive local laws. Dutchess has certified ag districts, but I did not confirm whether a given Pine Plains parcel is in one. Agritourism tied to farm-product sales and marketing can fall inside "farm operation."
- **Lodging is likely outside it.** Ag & Markets guidance lists bed and breakfast operations as "farm compatible businesses" regulated by other state and local rules, not as protected farm operations. A farm-stay STR therefore probably gets no §305-a protection from local STR or zoning rules.
- **Agritourism liability law.** NY's 2017 Safety in Agricultural Tourism Act (GOL Article 18, §§18-301 et seq.) limits liability for inherent risks if operators post warnings, train staff, and meet the statutory duties. It is not blanket immunity, and negligence is not shielded.

### Cited Findings
- **What §305-a requires.** Benefits apply to enterprises that (1) meet the state "farm operation" definition and (2) are located in a county-adopted, state-certified agricultural district. Ag & Markets compares local "farm" definitions to AML §301(11). — [NYS Ag & Markets farm operation guideline](https://agriculture.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2019/11/agguideline-farmoperation.pdf); [Cuddy & Feder, Hudson Valley start-up farms](https://www.cuddyfeder.com/agriculture-in-the-hudson-valley-start-up-farm-operations-and-receiving-the-benefits-of-the-agriculture-and-markets-law/)
- **Agritourism definition.** AML defines agritourism as "activities conducted by a farmer on-farm for the enjoyment and/or education of the public, which primarily promote the sale, marketing, production, harvesting or use of the farm's products." — [Cornell CALS agritourism](https://cals.cornell.edu/cornell-cooperative-extension/our-work/work-teams/agritourism); [AML text via Erie County](https://www3.erie.gov/agriculture/sites/www3.erie.gov.agriculture/files/2021-03/Agricultural%20Districts%20-%20Ag%20%26%20Markets%20Law.pdf)
- **B&Bs are "farm compatible," not protected.** An Ag & Markets presentation lists B&B operations and outdoor recreation as "farm compatible businesses" regulated under other state and local rules, not fully protected by the AML. — [Erie County / Ag & Markets presentation](https://www3.erie.gov/environment/sites/www3.erie.gov.environment/files/2022-05/agdistrictsandfarmops2_9-25-18.pdf) (search-summary reading; not fetched in full)
- **Pending bill.** S.1882 (2025–26 session) would add a new paragraph d to AML §305-a(1). I could not determine its content or status. — [Legiplex](https://app.legiplex.com/ny/legislature/2025/2025-r/bills/s01882/drafts-latest)
- **2017 agritourism liability law.**
  - It is the Safety in Agricultural Tourism Act, codified in the General Obligations Law.
  - Operators must post and maintain a "Warning to Visitors" sign about inherent risks and train employees.
  - Even compliance with §§18-303(a)–(e) may not fully insulate against suits. It is not blanket immunity.
  - Forms used in other states may not satisfy NY law.
  - Sources: [Farm and Dairy](https://www.farmanddairy.com/?p=453874); [Ag & Markets inherent-risk guidance (PDF)](https://agriculture.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2019/10/inherentriskguidanceforagritourismandpublic.pdf) (PDF text not extractable this session); [Barclay Damon / BH Law summary](https://www.bhlawpllc.com/the-new-york-safety-in-agriculture-tourism-act-finally-enacted/)

### Inferences
- **Protected vs. not.** On-farm activities that market farm products (u-pick, farm tours, farm-product sales) are the ones most likely to be protected under §305-a and covered by the GOL inherent-risk defense. Overnight lodging probably is neither.
- **Local rules still apply.** Expect Pine Plains zoning, building and fire code (Uniform Code), Dutchess DOH, and any town STR permit to govern the farmhouse rental.
- **Verify locally.** Check whether the parcel is in a certified Dutchess County Agricultural District. Ask Ag & Markets (Division of Land and Water Resources) for an informal opinion before relying on §305-a for any guest-facing use.

## 6. Entity structure: LLC for farm vs. STR; insurance

### Takeaway
- **Separate LLCs are the common practitioner approach.** One LLC holds or runs the farm and another holds the STR, possibly with land in a third holding entity leased to both. This isolates liability and keeps clean books for §183 and allocation.
- **Single-member LLCs don't change the tax result.** They are disregarded: Schedule F and E/C still flow to the 1040, and STR loophole and material participation results are unchanged.
- **Ag assessment and FSTC.** NY ag assessment and the Farmers' School Tax Credit have entity-specific rules. Rented land needs a written lease of 5 years or more, and an entity member living on the land counts as "owner's residence."
- **Insurance.** Standard farm-owner and homeowner policies typically exclude commercial STR use, so separate STR or commercial coverage is needed.

### Cited Findings
- **Rented land and ag assessment.** Rented land qualifies for ag assessment and can meet the requirement through a written rental agreement of at least 5 years. — [NYS DTF ag overview](https://www.tax.ny.gov/research/property/assess/valuation/ag_overview.htm)
- **Entity-member residence.** A shareholder, partner, or LLC member living on the land is deemed to occupy an owner's residence, not a tenant's. — [ORPTS legal opinion](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/orpts/legal_opinions/v3/113.htm) (via search summary)
- **Entity-level credit limits.** For partnerships and S corps, the new $20K farm donation credit limit applies at the entity level. — [Farm Credit East](https://www.farmcrediteast.com/resources/todays-harvest-Blog/260604FarmHighlightsNYSBudget)
- **Liability law and insurance cost.** The agritourism liability statute was enacted partly in response to rising insurance costs for farms hosting the public. — [Times Union blog](https://blog.timesunion.com/capitol/archives/278190/new-liability-law-aims-to-protect-new-yorks-agritourism-industry/)

### Inferences
- **Lease structure.** A common structure is a land LLC that leases the farmland to a farm LLC (5+ year written lease, which helps ag assessment) and the farmhouse to an STR LLC. Rents between commonly owned entities can trigger the §469 self-rental recharacterization rules, so a CPA should design this.
- **NY LLC publication requirement.** Each new NY LLC must publish formation notices, which can cost hundreds to over $1,000 depending on county [background; not verified this session]. In Dutchess, publication costs are generally moderate.
- **Insurance to quote:** a farm liability policy that includes an agritourism endorsement, a commercial or STR policy for the farmhouse, an umbrella, and workers' compensation if employees are hired, which is required in NY. Airbnb's AirCover is not a substitute for a primary policy.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced data on NY farm-stay insurance pricing or carrier availability.
- I found no authoritative source on LLC structuring for combined farm and STR operations. The guidance above is practitioner convention and inference.
